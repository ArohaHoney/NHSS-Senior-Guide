Step 1 — University Applications

Applying Through OUAC

The Ontario Universities' Application Centre is how you apply to every Ontario university. Here's everything you need to know.

⚠️
Use your personal email only. Your school account will be deactivated shortly after graduation. Create a Gmail if you don't have one. All university communications will go to this address.

What is OUAC?

OUAC is the centralized platform for submitting university applications across Ontario. Your guidance office automatically sends your grades — you do not submit transcripts, subapps, or extracurriculars through OUAC.

Key Dates

  • Portal opens: Late September – Early October
  • Deadline: January 15 — last day to submit interest in a program
  • Universities respond: typically 1–2 weeks after payment

How to Get Started

  1. Go to ouac.on.ca and create an account with your personal email.
  2. Click Undergraduate after logging in.
  3. Fill in Personal Details and My Background — double-check everything before submitting.
  4. Go to "My Choices" tab → click Add Program to select universities. You need at least 3.
  5. Go to Review and Payment. Green checkmarks = reviewed. Pay fees to officially submit.
  6. Wait for confirmation emails from universities (1–2 weeks). Then access their individual portals.
🚨
Your application is NOT sent until fees are paid. Universities will not receive anything until payment goes through on OUAC.

Step 2 — After You Apply

University Applicant Portals

After applying through OUAC, each university will email you a student/applicant number. Use it to access their portal — but wait for that email first.

✅
Wait for the email before trying to access any portal. If you haven't received anything after 2 weeks, contact the university's admissions office directly.

University of Toronto

Portal: JOIN U of T — used to track your application, upload documents, and receive decisions.

Step 1 — Activate Your JOINid

  • Go to the official JOIN U of T Portal and select "Enable your JOINid"
  • You'll need your Applicant Number, date of birth, and the email used on your application
  • Create your password and security information once activated

Step 2 — Log In & Monitor

  • Log in using your JOINid credentials (usually emailed a few days after applying)
  • Check application status, view supplementary requirements, and receive admission decisions
  • Access scholarship and residence information through the portal

Step 3 — Upload Documents (if required)

  • Transcripts, English proficiency scores, supplementary applications or profiles
  • Ontario high school students have grades sent automatically via OUAC
Open JOIN U of T Portal ↗

Grade 12 Courses

Course Difficulty Guide

Real ratings and advice from NHSS seniors. Your experience may vary depending on your teacher and strengths.

For each course, we chose a difficulty rating from 0–5, 5 being the hardest.

  • These ratings are based purely off our opinions, and could vary based on your strengths.
  • Courses also vary depending on teachers — not all teachers mark or teach the same.
  • If you would like to know more information on which teachers to look out for, message us directly on Instagram @nhss.seniorguides and we'll try our best to respond as soon as possible.

⚠️ All info is based on the 2026 curriculum from our personal experience. Material or difficulty may change over time.

ENG4U
English
4.5 / 5
★★ ★★ ★

Extremely time-consuming with subjective marking. Getting above 90 is very doable — 95+ in-person is almost unheard of. Favoritism is real.

Top Tips
  • Do it in summer school or night school if possible
  • Ask for feedback on every assignment — teachers notice and reward it
  • Be active in class (not annoying)
  • Use Grammarly or AI for spelling/clarity checks only — don't write with AI
Course Material
  • Writing quizzes and tests
  • Book club: 1984, Vox, Brave New World
  • Essay writing (multiple)
  • Shakespearean writing — in-class readings help
  • Culminating + Exam: both based on the Shakespeare book your class read
MHF4U
Advanced Functions
3.5 / 5
★★ ★ ★★

Builds on Grade 11 Functions. Most students find it easier than Functions. Teacher-dependent — effort matters more than raw ability.

Top Tips
  • Ask questions — no question is too dumb
  • Always do the test review packages they give you
  • Understand concepts before memorizing procedures
  • JensenMath & Organic Chemistry Tutor on YouTube are great but use class methods for marks
MCV4U
Calculus & Vectors
3.5 / 5
★★ ★ ★★

Requires Advanced Functions. Many consider this the hardest math, but it's very doable with strong work ethic. Do problems until you feel confident before every test.

Top Tips
  • Advanced Functions (MHF4U) is a prerequisite
  • Same advice as Advanced Functions — review packages, ask questions
  • JensenMath is great, but stick to class methods
MDM4U
Data Management
2.5 / 5
★★ ★★★

Easiest of the three math options. Highly recommended if your program requires any math. With decent effort, 90+ is very achievable.

Top Tips
  • Don't get cocky — still put in effort for each assessment
  • Consistent work ethic makes 90+ very achievable
SCH4U
Chemistry
4.5 / 5
★★ ★★ ★

Nothing like Grade 11 Chem — much harder. Requires conceptual understanding, not just memorization. Lots of application. Cheat sheets allowed for labs and some units.

Reviewed by Nicole Yanovsky, UofT Chemical Engineering Student

Student Experience
  • Challenging, but lets you go outside your comfort zone and learn how products and processes actually work — which is genuinely interesting
Study Tips
  • Practice a lot — do all the homework you can and ask questions when you need help understanding
  • Do the sample tests that are provided
Additional Advice
  • Past tests/quizzes are extremely helpful — ask around until you get them. Way more worth doing than the homework.
  • Ask questions in class and during tests when confused
  • Make friends with strong students in your class
  • Plickers (class quizzes) = 5% of your final grade — the class can collaborate, so worth attending
  • NotebookLM/Gemini are great for understanding concepts and quizzing yourself
  • Bozeman Science and Orgo Chem Tutor on YouTube are great for understanding concepts
SBI4U
Biology
4.5 / 5
★★ ★★ ★

Lots of memorization and detail. Far more specific than Grade 11. You can lose marks quickly if you miss any keywords — be methodical and thorough in answers.

Reviewed by Sophie Low, McMaster Health Science Student

Student Experience
  • Sophie really loved bio — it's what pushed her to pursue McMaster Health Science
  • The teacher can make a huge difference — having Ms. Szoke gave her the confidence to apply and go for it
Study Tips
  • Review the slideshows provided, then create summary sheets — one sheet per slideshow max
  • If you can't condense a slideshow onto one sheet, it means you haven't fully learned it yet
Additional Advice
  • Review slides after class — prioritize understanding over memorization
  • Past tests/quizzes are extremely helpful — ask around until you get them
  • Spam keywords on short answer questions and think carefully when answering
  • Ask questions in class and during tests when confused
  • Textbook questions take ~30 mins and help a lot — use AI for answer keys since there's none provided
  • NotebookLM/Gemini are great for understanding concepts and self-quizzing before tests
  • The Amoeba Sisters on YouTube has great videos for understanding concepts
SPH4U
Physics
4.5 / 5
★★ ★★ ★

Completely different from Grade 11 Physics. Requires significant time and deep understanding. Only take it if your university program requires it.

Reviewed by Avin Karimi, Waterloo Mechanical Engineering Student

Student Experience
  • Tough mentally — heavy workload throughout the course
  • Some of the most fun labs and in-class activities of any STEM course
  • More abstract than other STEM courses — requires deeper conceptual understanding
  • Uses university-level lab concepts like linearization during labs
Key Warnings
  • Not recommended if below 75% in Grade 11 Physics
  • Only take if required for your program
  • Very doable with hard work — and great prep for university physics
Study Tips
  • Focus on reviewing lessons, watching videos, and using external sources alongside the textbook
  • Do many problems until you deeply understand the concepts — don't just memorize steps
ICS4U
Computer Science
2 / 5
★★ ★★★

Reuses ICS3U concepts but in Java instead of Python. If you did well in Grade 11, expect similar results here.

Reviewed by Kennedy Tran, UofT Computer Science Student

Student Experience
  • Not a very difficult course overall
  • If you did well in Grade 11 CS, you should expect similar results here
Study Tips
  • The learning blog (required throughout the course) summarizes all concepts — use it to study for tests
  • Cheat sheets are allowed later in the course — take advantage of them
TEJ4M
Computer Engineering
1.5 / 5
★ ★★★★

Builds directly on Grade 11 computer engineering concepts. Straightforward if you did well in Grade 11.

Reviewed by Kennedy Tran, UofT Computer Science Student

Student Experience
  • Not very difficult — reuses concepts from Grade 11 computer engineering
  • If you did well in Grade 11, expect similar results here
Study Tips
  • Review the worksheets and practice tests given in class — that's all you really need
TER4M
Robotics
2.5 / 5
★★ ★★★

Real robotics challenges with real deadlines. Enjoyable but would benefit from more structure.

Reviewed by Timothy Lin, UWaterloo Mechanical Engineering Student

Student Experience
  • Gives students real robotics challenges and deadlines to meet for a competition-ready robot
  • Enjoyable course — would like to see more structure added in the future
Study Tips
  • Quizzes/tests are based on the game manual — know it well
  • Use NotebookLM to create flashcards for rote memorization
  • Watch matches to get a better feel for the game

Know Before You Apply

Explore Your Options

Two essential tools every Grade 12 student should know about before locking in their university choices.

🏛️

Ontario Universities' Fair

Talk to universities face-to-face — for free

✅ Free to attend 📅 Sept 26–27, 2026 · 9:30 AM – 5:00 PM

What is it?

Every Ontario university in one massive room. Walk up and ask real questions to university reps and students about programs, campus life, averages, costs, and more.

Where?

  • Metro Toronto Convention Centre (MTCC) — downtown, right next to the CN Tower
  • The fair is held in the South Building (222 Bremner Blvd) — closer to the lake and the Aquarium
  • Enter the North Building (255 Front St W) and use the skywalk to get there without going outside

Why go?

  • Ask one-on-one questions with university reps about specific programs
  • Pick up booklets covering programs, competitive averages, costs, and entrance scholarships
  • Get a feel for schools you're considering before committing

Tips before you go

  1. Register for a pass at ouevents.ca (registration typically opens in August). When a university scans your QR code, you'll automatically get their emails and brochures.
  2. Skip your bag — they give you one for free to carry all the booklets.
  3. Hit the big schools first. Larger schools get crowded fast — arrive early and visit them before the rush.
  4. Wear comfortable shoes. You're on concrete floors for hours — your feet will thank you.
🔍

OUInfo

Ontario's official university program database

✅ Free · Official OUAC resource

What is it?

OUInfo (formerly eINFO) is the official online guide to all Ontario universities, managed by OUAC. It's the most reliable database for finding program info, requirements, and costs — way more trustworthy than random Reddit threads.

Program Finder

  • Search every undergraduate program offered in Ontario
  • Filter by keywords, program type (e.g. engineering, nursing), university, campus, or co-op availability

Admission Requirements

  • See the exact Grade 12 prerequisite courses required for any program
  • Check admission and recommended averages — note: recommended averages are usually lower than actual acceptance averages
  • Find out if a supplementary application is required

Comparison Tool

  • Compare programs side-by-side across universities — tuition fees, grade requirements, enrollment sizes

Campus Overview

  • Residence availability and costs
  • Campus culture, student population, extracurriculars
  • Scholarships, bursaries, and tuition estimates
